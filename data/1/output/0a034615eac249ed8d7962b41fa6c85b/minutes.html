Community Conflict Resolution Meeting on Property Management and General Meeting of Homeowners

General Minutes

Executive Summary

The meeting convened homeowners, the Homeowners Committee, property management representatives, and a subdistrict government official to address escalating community disputes over unpaid property fees, service deficiencies, and the demand to convene a General Meeting of Homeowners to vote on property management. Homeowners, led by Speaker 4, detailed persistent service failures since handover, including unlit gatepost lights, non-functional water features, damaged surveillance cameras, and inadequate staffing. Property management, represented by Speaker 2 and Manager Wu (Speaker 7), countered with financial constraints, citing over 1 million in unpaid fees, operational losses, and developer-related infrastructure issues, while threatening legal action against the Homeowners Committee for alleged procedural illegalities. The subdistrict official urged both sides to de-escalate and improve communication. The meeting concluded without consensus: homeowners insisted on proceeding with the General Meeting, while property management committed to filing a lawsuit this month. Both sides agreed to pursue legal and procedural avenues.

Key Discussion Points

1. Payment Arrears and Financial Disputes

  • Unpaid property fees: Manager Wu (Speaker 7) stated that homeowners owe the Property Management Company over 1 million, specifically citing figures of 1.7 million and 1.4 million.
  • Operational losses: Manager Wu reported that the company has been operating at a loss, with the loss margin widening this year.
  • Fee collection challenges: Speaker 2 referenced payment collection issues, including a project over 10 years old and 20 households without employees from tomorrow.
  • Developer-related infrastructure costs: Discussion covered the connection fee that should have been paid by the developer but was not completed or inspected; all fees, including the infrastructure auxiliary fee, totaled 900 million.
  • Phase-specific subsidies: Phase Two was expected to receive a subsidy up to 29%; phase one reached over a thousand five, then 17 or 18 percent, with 11-point-something percent also mentioned.
  • Payment demands: Manager Wu mentioned demanding 400,000 back before the New Year to fix part of the issues.
  • Bankruptcy context: Manager Wu stated that the real estate company is filing for bankruptcy.

2. Homeowners Committee Formation and General Meeting Demand

  • Online petition: Over 200 homeowners signed an online sign-up list demanding a General Meeting of Homeowners to vote on property management.
  • Committee structure: The Homeowners Committee working group is the only group, with prospective homeowners split into 5 groups; the preparatory group had 6 people, each contacting a portion.
  • Group history: The earliest homeowners group was set up by Old Qiu and has existed for 5-6 years.
  • Homeowner concerns: 11 problems were raised by homeowners.
  • Eligibility disputes: Speaker 2 argued that people in arrears cannot serve as homeowner representatives, and people using someone else's name also cannot serve.
  • Homeowner organization: Homeowners organized spontaneously and recruited 130,000; Phase Two and Phase Three combined had only about 20 households signed up for quality one; originally 50 households, reached 10; 20 or 30 homeowners want culture change.

3. Detailed Listing of Community Service Deficiencies

  • Gatepost lights: Courtyard gatepost lights at every household's entrance have never been turned on.
  • Water features: Internal water features have never been turned on since homes were sold; the welcome water feature at the entrance has also never been turned on.
  • Surveillance cameras: Cameras are almost all damaged, with essentially no working cameras; last month thieves entered the community and police could not retrieve data from the cameras.
  • Monitoring staffing: The monitoring room should have someone on duty 24 hours a day, but this is not the case.
  • Additional issues: Speaker 3 mentioned water cut off every few days, water seepage, courtyard wall issues, and snow not cleared.

4. Legal Threats and Counterarguments

  • Property management legal action: Speaker 2 stated that a lawsuit will be filed this month against the Homeowners Committee and related parties, citing gathered evidence of procedural illegalities and arrears.
  • Court communications: Speaker 2 mentioned communicating with the Higher People's Court this morning at 9:52, and calling Director Gao and Bincheng District People's Court.
  • Arrears lawsuits: Homeowners whose arrears period exceeds one year will be sued.
  • Homeowner countersuit: Speaker 8 stated they will countersue and produce evidence if sued.
  • Defamation concerns: Speaker 8 noted rumors are being spread about ties to property management, and they are willing to provide evidence and face scrutiny.
  • Property management defense: Speaker 2 stated the company has gathered evidence and grounds within the homeowners group, arguing the Homeowners Committee was established with illegalities.

5. Mediation Attempts and Communication Gaps

  • Subdistrict official's recommendation: Speaker 3 suggested that property management should show real action to win homeowners' hearts, noting that conflicts are piling up and getting sharper.
  • Property management self-reflection: Speaker 2 emphasized the need for self-reflection by property management.
  • Communication with developer: Manager Wu stated they have told the real estate company no less than five or six times, possibly ten times, regarding outstanding issues.
  • Meeting conclusion: Speaker 2 stated that today's outcome is not great and suggested ending the meeting; Speaker 4 insisted on proceeding with the General Meeting of Homeowners.

Decisions & Rulings

  • General Meeting of Homeowners: The General Meeting of Homeowners will be convened as soon as possible to allow all homeowners to vote on property management; the Homeowners Committee is responsible for ensuring compliance with legal procedures and voter eligibility requirements, coordinating with the subdistrict office for guidance.
  • Property Management Lawsuit: Property Management will file a lawsuit this month against the Homeowners Committee and related parties, based on gathered evidence of procedural illegalities and arrears; coordination with the legal department and courts is required.
  • Homeowner Countersuit: Homeowners will countersue if sued, producing evidence including documentation of service deficiencies and unmet commitments; coordination with legal counsel is required upon receipt of the lawsuit.
  • Ongoing Mediation: The subdistrict office will continue mediation efforts, urging both sides to improve communication and property management to demonstrate tangible service improvements; timeline is ongoing.
  • No Consensus Reached: The meeting concluded without a consensus, with both sides agreeing to pursue legal and procedural avenues.
